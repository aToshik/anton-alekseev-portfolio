How I turned a small business CRM into one conversation

8 → 1 · request types, one thread CRM → Chat · one cycle from request to payment

Figma · Heap · FullStory

My role

Sole product designer. I designed the feature from scratch, end to end: concept, flows, UI for web and the hybrid mobile app, interactive prototypes, handoff and QA. I worked with the PM and the dev team.

Context

One owner, many doors

Camilyo is an all-in-one platform for small service businesses: salons, studios, coaches, consultants. It covers the website, bookings and classes, sales (estimates, invoices, payments), marketing, presence on Google and Facebook, and a CRM, on web and mobile. Customers reach a business through all of these doors: a booking on the site, a contact form, an estimate request, a call-me-back, a phone call, a Google review, a Facebook message, email or SMS.

Problem

Every request lived in its own module

Each request type looked different and came with a different amount of information and different actions. To handle one customer, the owner jumped between Calendar, Sales, Presence and Marketing, and opened the CRM every time they needed context: past requests, bookings, products, services.

Slow repliesthe owner had to find the request first, then the module that could act on it.
Lost contextthe customer's history sat in the CRM, away from the conversation.
Lost dealsthe moment a customer was ready to book or pay passed while the owner switched screens.

Insight

Owners already know how to run a conversation

Everyone runs their life in messengers: WhatsApp, Messenger, Telegram. A small business owner doesn't need a new tool to learn. So we didn't build a request manager. We used the chat pattern everyone already knows, and put the business objects inside the conversation.

Key decisions

From separate features to one cycle

Alerts that needed the owner were scattered across modules. Inbox gathers them into one thread per customer, so every step of the deal happens in the same place: request, booking or estimate, invoice, payment, review.

01 · One customer, one thread, every door

A salon gets bookings, a contractor gets estimate requests, a shop gets orders, and all of them get forms, calls, reviews and messages. Every request lands in the customer's thread with its source ("Sent from Women's Haircut page at 15:45"). The owner replies in the same composer and picks the channel the customer wrote in: Email, SMS, WhatsApp or Messenger. "Show pending only" leaves just what still needs an answer. Rejected: an inbox per channel, like most messaging tools, and a queue of requests, like a ticket system. Both split one customer into pieces, and the owner lost the history again.

02 · One card for every request, with a lifecycle

Booking, class, estimate, invoice, order, form, call-me-back and review share one card: source, title, status, key details and the next action. The card is a live object. A booking moves from Pending to Accepted, Completed, No-show or Declined in place, and every change leaves a one-line event in the thread: rescheduled, reassigned, paid. Clicking an event scrolls back to its card and highlights it. One anatomy also means the owner learns every request type once. Rejected: a new message for every status change. The thread filled up with copies of the same booking, so the card changes in place and short events keep the history.

03 · From estimate to payment without leaving the chat

Kenneth asks for a lawn care quote through the site. The owner opens the estimate builder over the thread, with the request and contact already filled in, adds the service and sends it with a personal note. The estimate card follows the deal: Sent, Approved, Invoiced. One click turns the approved estimate into an invoice, and the deposit and the final payment land as events in the same thread. Contact details open next to the chat, so the CRM comes to the conversation, not the other way around. Rejected: sending the owner to the Sales module, which broke the conversation, and a full builder inside a chat bubble, which was too heavy. The builder opens over the chat, and the estimate turns into the invoice, so nobody types the same items twice.

04 · Answer with the business, not just words

Most questions are "how much", "when are you free" or "do you have it". The composer shares the business itself: a product or service page with its price, a coupon, a team member or a saved reply with variables like {CustomerName}. Daniel asks on Facebook about running shoes in his size, the owner attaches the product page with a short note, and his order comes back to the same thread as a card. Rejected: a plain text answer with a price. It started another round of questions, while a shared product page is already a way to buy.

Edge cases

A chat that still works when the data doesn't

Real businesses start empty, serve groups and switch devices all day, so the thread had to hold up outside the happy path.

Empty startA new account sees "One way for all the customers requests" and can import contacts from Google.
No channelIf a customer has no email or phone, the composer says so and offers another channel.
Group threadsA class with several participants is one thread with stacked avatars.
Every screenAdaptive layouts from 1024 to 1920 px, and the same Inbox in the hybrid React Native app for iOS and Android.

Impact

CRM in a conversational UI

Design8 → 1request types in one thread, with one card anatomy
Qualitative5 modules → 1 chatCalendar, Sales, Presence, Marketing and CRM in one conversation
QualitativeWeb + iOS + Androidone experience on every platform

We didn't track exact numbers for Inbox. The change is in the workflow: before, every request had its own screen and its own rules. After, every request is a card in a conversation and ends with a next action. Owners accept bookings, quote, invoice and get paid from the chat, with the customer's history right next to it, and open the Calendar only for detailed changes.

Reflection

The best interface was the one people already used. The hard part wasn't the chat. It was making every business object behave the same inside it.

A chat was easy to agree on. The real work was the card: one anatomy and one lifecycle for eight very different requests, so the owner never had to learn a new screen. Once every object spoke the same language, joining separate features into one cycle stopped being an integration problem and became a conversation.
