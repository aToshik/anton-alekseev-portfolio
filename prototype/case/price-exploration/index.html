Work/Price Exploration

Optimization · AI · Self-service · B2B  |  Beacon, Kissterra · publicly "AI Bidding Optimization" · sole product designer · InsurTech

How I turned a black-box pricing algorithm into a bidding tool buyers trust

$8M → $30M+ · monthly media investment 7% MoM · growth in policy binds

Figma · Hotjar · Mixpanel

My role

Sole product designer, end to end: research, UX and UI, handoff and QA, from the first version to cross-campaign scale. I worked daily with the PM, a data analyst and engineers.

Context

Buying insurance leads in a live auction

Beacon is the real-time bidding (RTB) product inside Kissterra's MarketingOS. Media buyers at top U.S. insurance carriers use it to buy leads in live auctions, across dozens of channels and hundreds of segments. Price here is non-linear: a 5% bid increase can bring 1% more leads or 10%.

Problem

An algorithm that worked, and buyers who didn't act on it

Buyers tested prices by hand: duplicating ad groups, changing bids, comparing results across separate views. It was slow, didn't scale past a few segments, and led to two expensive failure modes.

Overpayingon traffic that doesn't convert.
Underinvestingbidding too low and leaving quality traffic on the table.

On a large cohort, one wrong price could cost hundreds of thousands of dollars a month. Our data science team had an engine that tests prices on a share of traffic and predicts the win rate at each one. Buyers still didn't act on it, because they couldn't see why it recommended what it did.

Insight

Not how to automate bidding, but how to earn the buyer's trust

Session recordings and customer interviews showed buyers sometimes bid against the numbers on purpose, for example entering a new segment aggressively at a temporary loss. The algorithm can't know that intent. So the design had to show the evidence and leave the decision with the buyer.

Key decisions

From black box to a tool buyers trust

01Set up in context 02Every result 03Business objects
01 · Set up tests where the context already lives

Buyers start a test right in the Ad Groups table, their daily workspace. It already shows the full performance data they decide on, and its filters and bulk actions let them select dozens of ad groups at once. The dedicated Bidding Optimization view does one job: visualize the running test and the recommendation once it finishes. Opt-in per ad group kept buyers in control while trust was built. Rejected: a separate setup flow (pick ad groups, then configure the test). It would rebuild selection from scratch and strip away the data buyers decide with.

02 · Show every result, not just the best one

We could have shown only the sweet point, one recommended price. Instead each test plots all its testing points with predicted win rate, lead volume and CPL, and marks the sweet point among them. In practice buyers often picked a price the data rated lower, because it fit their strategy. That led to the Score Method: buyers choose whether "best" means performance (including ROE) or auction competitiveness only. Rejected: one recommended price change, with the rest of the data hidden.

03 · Scale to the objects the business thinks in

Once the Cross-CampaignCross-Campaign ManagementHow I let buyers edit thousands of campaigns as oneHours → Min · bulk updates infrastructure was ready, buyers could test Bidding Groups (same-segment ad groups across campaigns, one bid price) or Channel Groups (similar channels, one bid multiplier). The algorithm got many times more data, so recommendations got more accurate and far fewer tests ended in "insufficient data".

Endless tests followed, and some buyers ran them only to watch auction competition in key segments. The tool had become a way to read the market. That is probably why the client called it "Price Exploration" in its communication with us, though it officially shipped as AI Bidding Optimization.

Impact

Buyers trusted it, and moved budget onto the platform

7% MoMgrowth in policy binds
QualitativeRight price, both waysless overpaying, less quality traffic left on the table
QualitativeManual → algorithmicbuyers shifted almost entirely from hand-set bids

Buyers moved almost entirely from manual bidding to Price Exploration. With prices backed by tested data, the client shifted spend into RTB through Beacon from other channels such as Google Ads, and named bidding optimization at scale as the main driver.

Reflection

Trust first, autopilot later. One-click automation only works after buyers could see and check everything.

The product has since moved to one-click apply, with the platform taking over. That works only because buyers first had a version where they could see and check everything. If I started again, I'd push for the cross-campaign infrastructure earlier. We led with the more impressive feature, then hit technical limits exactly when the client wanted scale.
