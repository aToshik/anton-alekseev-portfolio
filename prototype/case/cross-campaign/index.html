Work/Cross-Campaign Management

Workflow · Infra · B2B  |  Beacon, Kissterra · sole product designer · InsurTech

How I let buyers edit thousands of campaigns as one

Hours → Min · large-scale updates Every campaign · one workspace

Figma · Hotjar

My role

Sole product designer, end to end: concept, prototypes, rounds of client feedback, handoff and QA. I worked daily with the PM and engineers.

Context

Dozens of campaigns, thousands of prices

Beacon is Kissterra's real-time bidding product for top U.S. insurance carriers. A large account runs dozens of campaigns. Each one holds its own ad groups (audience segments with a bid price) and channels (traffic sources with a bid multiplier). The auction moves every day, so buyers change prices every day.

Problem

Every change lived inside one campaign

To change the price of one segment everywhere, a buyer opened each campaign, made the change there and saved, then moved to the next one. There was no list of all ad groups or all channels, so there was also no way to compare them or find the ones that needed a change.

Hours of clicksone strategic update meant repeating the same edit campaign by campaign.
Errors at scaleone missed campaign kept the old price and quietly kept spending.

Insight

The campaign is one dimension, not a container

Client feedback and Hotjar sessions showed buyers think in segments and traffic sources, not in campaigns. The campaign was just where the data happened to be stored. So the first job was visibility: every ad group and channel in one place. Managing them at scale came after that.

Key decisions

From one campaign at a time to the whole account at once

02 · Group what the business manages as one

Buyers assign ad groups from different campaigns to a Bidding Group with one bid price. Channels work the same way in Channel Groups with one multiplier. Both tables got two views: Flat lists every object, Grouped folds them under their group, so buyers scan and steer at group level and expand only when they need detail. 03 · Set rules once for every campaign

Bid modifiers (by state, device, homeowner, age and more) and filters used to be set campaign by campaign. Cross-Campaign Settings became the top level of the hierarchy: filters and modifiers for the whole account, with Import and Export. Campaigns inherit them and add more detailed rules of their own. Buyers do most of their tuning at the campaign level, while managers use Cross-Campaign Settings when something changes at a strategic or regulatory level.

Reflection

Scale needs layers, not just bulk. Bulk actions made changes fast. Control came from the hierarchy around them.

A table of every object was only useful once buyers could filter and act on it. Speed came from bulk actions and groups, but trust in account-wide changes came from layers: rules at the top, groups in the middle, campaign detail at the bottom. Buyers could change everything at once and still keep the exceptions they cared about.
