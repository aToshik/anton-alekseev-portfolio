Work/Channel Analysis

Data Viz · AI · Self-service  |  Beacon, Kissterra · sole product designer · B2B RTB, US insurance · 2,000+ channels · 2024

How I turned silent channel decay into a one-click decision

Weeks → Hours · detection time 2,000+ · channels monitored

Figma · Figma Make (interactive prototype) · AI for feedback synthesis

My role

Sole designer on a cross-functional team (PM, engineers), end to end, from the detection concept to the dedicated Channel Analysis interface.

Context

A bidding platform where every channel is a budget line

Beacon is Kissterra's B2B platform for buying insurance leads in real time, built for top US carriers. Media buyers manage bids and budgets across hundreds to thousands of traffic channels per client, each with its own price, volume and lead quality. Their job is to keep money flowing to the channels that convert.

Problem

Channels decay silently — at scale, that's weeks of wasted spend

Channel performance isn't static: a channel that delivered quality leads for months can quietly degrade while a buyer keeps spending on it. At scale (up to 2,000 channels per client), low-volume channels go unwatched, so weeks pass before anyone notices and the budget damage is already done.

Insight

Not more data — the right data, in context

Clients didn't need more data; they needed the right data, in context. And the hardest call wasn't building the big tool, it was knowing where to start.

Key decisions

Four phases, smallest surface first

01Inline 02Popover 03Feedback loop 04Dedicated UI
01 · Inline indicators (MVP)

I surfaced the signal right in the table: color by direction of change (red = degrading, green = improving, per each metric's higher-is-better or lower-is-better rule) plus a chevron for magnitude (one to three arrows). Prove value and learn first. Rejected: building the full interface on day one.

02 · Popover insight

The signal moved from metrics to the channel. One click opens an explainable insight: which KPIs flagged, how it hits overall performance, and where money is leaking or under-invested. Three insight types — Invest, Reduce, Pause — with Invest/Reduce graded by priority (change strength, volume, business impact). I prototyped the interaction in Figma Make to test the UX live: thumbs up/down, note vs none, actions vs none, and the aftermath (feedback toast, action-confirmation toast, whether the flag persists or clears).

03 · Feedback loop — signature process

Thumbs up/down and a note on every insight captured the signal; I ran it through AI for synthesis. The pattern: most rejects clustered on new channels with thin data, low volume, or a recent price change, where buyers wanted to give the channel time. That drove concrete changes to the interface and the algorithm: channel change history, and metrics split into drivers, context & risk, and supporting evidence. On actions: Accept applies a recommendation in one click; Reject opens a feedback field.

04 · Dedicated interface

Once value was proven, a dedicated interface with deeper detail: primary-driver vs context metrics, risks, supporting evidence, recent channel changes, a confidence score, and the projected spend impact of an action (single or bulk).

Edge cases / Uncertainty

The algorithm flags; the human always decides

The signal carries meaning before any click: color shows direction, chevrons show magnitude. Before acting, the buyer sees a confidence score and the projected spend impact; Accept applies in one click, Reject captures why. The algorithm flags and recommends, the human always decides.

Impact

Faster detection, daily use, less wasted spend

Reflection

The hardest design problem isn't building a complex feature, it's knowing where to start. One small surface, the popover, made everything else make sense.
